# Visualizing radiative transfer I: Specific Intensity | 23.11.2026

**Light** is the main messanger of the stars. Through it, we, astrophysicists, can understand from the depths of the cosmos to the hearts of stars. More than just poetic words, the quantitative understanding of how light -- a.k.a. electromagnetic radiation -- travels through and interact with matter is fundamental to essentially all areas of Astronomy, and beyond (e.g., in climate sciences). In fact, ``radiative transfer'' (which I will call it RT) is present in our very daily life all the time. For instance, right now, as you read this text, or feel the warmth of the Sun or a hot cup of coffee on your skin!

Scenery descriptions aside -- or not -- RT it is part of the mandatory disciplines of most Astronomy/Astrophysics bechelors, master, and PhD programs. 
Despite being so fundamental, and full of very good dedicated textbooks to teach it, I -- and many other people -- have had always a hard time trying to grasp it. The principle and main goal of RT is actually ridiculously simple. We just want to answer one question: ``What happens to the **light** as it travels through **this** path?''
